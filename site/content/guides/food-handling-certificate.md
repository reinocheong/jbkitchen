## What Is the Food Handling Certificate?

The **Food Handling Certificate** (*Sijil Kursus Pengendali Makanan*) is a mandatory certification for every person who handles food in Malaysia. It is required under **Regulation 30 of the Food Hygiene Regulations 2009**, enforced by the Ministry of Health Malaysia (KKM).

The certificate proves that a person has completed an accredited training course on basic food hygiene and safe food handling practices. It is **non-negotiable** — operating a food business without certified handlers is an offence.

The certificate is valid for **5 years** from the date of issue and must be renewed before expiry.

## Who Needs the Food Handling Certificate?

Under the law, a **"food handler"** is defined broadly. You need this certificate if your job involves:

- **Kitchen staff** — chefs, cooks, prep cooks, kitchen assistants
- **Service staff** — waiters, waitresses, counter staff who serve food
- **Food delivery riders** — anyone transporting prepared food to customers
- **Frozen food handlers** — warehouse staff, pickers, packers, delivery drivers
- **Central kitchen workers** — even if food is only cooked and packed for distribution
- **Market stall operators** — pasar malam, food truck, and bazaar vendors
- **Catering staff** — on-site and off-site event food handlers

### Minimum Requirement per Outlet

Each food premises must have **at least one certified food handler** present during all operating hours. However, KKM strongly recommends — and many local councils now require — that **all** staff who touch food hold the certificate.

## How to Get the Food Handling Certificate

The process is straightforward and can be completed within **1 to 2 days**.

### Step 1: Find an Accredited Training Provider

The course must be conducted by a training provider **recognised by KKM**. You can choose between:

- **In-person courses** — held at training centres or on-site at your premises
- **Online courses** — increasingly popular, especially since 2020

A full list of registered providers is available on the [KKM Food Safety and Quality Division website](https://fsq.moh.gov.my).

### Step 2: Attend the Training (3–4 Hours)

The course covers:

- Food safety and hygiene principles
- Causes of food poisoning and contamination
- Personal hygiene requirements
- Safe food storage and temperature control (-18°C frozen, 0–4°C chilled)
- Cleaning and sanitisation
- Pest control basics
- Food handling regulations in Malaysia

The course is conducted in **Bahasa Malaysia**, though some providers offer English or Mandarin sessions.

### Step 3: Pass the Assessment

At the end of the course, you take a short **multiple-choice test** (typically 20–30 questions). The passing mark is usually **70%**. Most people pass on the first attempt — the material is practical and straightforward.

### Step 4: Receive Your Certificate

Upon passing, you receive:

- A physical certificate card (laminated)
- Your name registered in the KKM database

Keep the certificate on the premises — KKM officers may ask to see it during inspections.

## Validity and Renewal

- **Validity**: 5 years from the date of issue
- **Renewal**: Retake the course and assessment before expiry
- **Grace period**: No grace period — an expired certificate is invalid immediately

Set a calendar reminder for **3 months before expiry** to schedule renewal training.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

If KKM officers find food handlers without valid certificates during an inspection:

- **Individual fine**: Up to RM10,000 or imprisonment up to 2 years (under Food Act 1983)
- **Business compound**: RM500–5,000 per uncertified handler
- **Closure order**: Premises may be closed under Section 11 of the Food Act
- **Impact on other certifications**: MeSTI, HACCP, and halal certification all require certified food handlers

## FAQ

### Can I take the food handling course online?

**Yes.** Many KKM-accredited providers offer fully online courses. You watch the training videos, complete the assessment online, and receive your certificate by post or courier. Online courses typically cost **RM50–80** and take around **3–4 hours** to complete — you can pause and resume at your own pace. Ensure the provider is on the KKM-approved list before enrolling.

### How long is the course?

The standard course is **3 to 4 hours**, including the training session and the final assessment. Some providers split this over two days, but most deliver it in a single half-day session. Online courses can be completed at your own pace within the provider's access period (usually 7–30 days).

### What happens if my staff don't have the certificate?

During a KKM inspection, the officer will check food handling certificates. If staff are found working without valid certificates, you will be issued a compound notice (fine) and given a deadline — typically **14 days** — to get all staff certified. Repeat offences can lead to higher fines, prosecution, and closure of the premises.

### Is the certificate the same as typhoid vaccination?

**No.** The food handling certificate and typhoid vaccination are two separate requirements:

- **Food Handling Certificate** — training and assessment on food safety
- **Typhoid vaccination** — a medical requirement for all food handlers

You need **both**. The typhoid vaccination must be renewed every **3 years** from any registered clinic. Cost is approximately **RM20–40 per person**.

### Do part-time staff and interns need the certificate?

**Yes.** The law applies to anyone who handles food, regardless of employment status — full-time, part-time, contract, intern, or family member helping in a family business. If they touch food served to the public, they must be certified.

### Can one person cover multiple outlets?

**No.** Each food premises must have at least one certified handler present at that specific location during operating hours. If you operate three restaurant branches, you need at least three certified handlers — one per branch.

### Do food delivery riders for GrabFood or Foodpanda need the certificate?

Technically, delivery riders are food handlers under the broad definition. In practice, KKM enforcement currently focuses on food **preparation** premises rather than third-party delivery riders. However, if you employ your own delivery drivers (company riders), they should hold the certificate.
