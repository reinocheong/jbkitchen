Cold Storage Temperature Standards: Frozen Food Guide

Legal Requirements Under Food Hygiene Regulations 2009

In Malaysia, cold storage for frozen food is regulated under the Food Hygiene Regulations 2009, enforced by the Ministry of Health (KKM) through its Food Safety and Quality Division (BKKM). The regulations set mandatory temperature standards for storing and transporting food to prevent spoilage and foodborne illness.

Failure to comply is not a minor issue. During a KKM inspection, if your cold storage temperatures are outside the legal range, you can face:

  • Compound fines up to RM10,000
  • Seizure and destruction of non-compliant stock
  • Suspension or revocation of your food business license
  • Closure order under Section 11 of the Food Act 1983

Temperature Zones — The Three Standards

ZoneTemperatureApplies ToKKM Compliance
Frozen≤ -18°CFrozen meat, poultry, seafood, processed frozen food, ice creamMandatory
Chilled0°C to 4°CFresh meat, dairy, prepared food awaiting cooking, chilled ready mealsMandatory
Ambient / DryRoom temperature (<30°C suggested)Canned goods, dry ingredients, packaging materialsBest practice

Frozen Zone: ≤ -18°C

This is the single most important number in frozen food storage. The -18°C standard is not arbitrary:

  • At -18°C, bacterial growth is effectively halted
  • Enzyme activity in food is minimised (though not eliminated)
  • Ice crystal formation is stable — minimises texture damage
  • Freezer burn risk is lower than at warmer temperatures

Critical detail: The regulation requires the core temperature of the food to be at or below -18°C, not just the air temperature in the freezer. The air temperature may need to be set lower (e.g., -20°C to -22°C) to ensure food core temperature is maintained, especially during door openings and stock rotation.

Chilled Zone: 0°C to 4°C

The danger zone for food is 5°C to 60°C — this is where bacteria multiply rapidly. Chilled storage at 0–4°C:

  • Slows bacterial growth dramatically
  • Keeps fresh food safe for days, not hours
  • Is mandatory for fresh meat, poultry, dairy, and prepared foods

Never set your chiller to 5°C or above — the 4°C upper limit is strict under Malaysian regulations.

Ice Cream and Specialty Frozen

For ice cream and frozen desserts, the industry standard is stricter:

  • ≤ -25°C — ideal for long-term ice cream storage
  • At -18°C, ice cream develops larger ice crystals and loses texture quality faster
  • KKM may accept -18°C legally, but commercial ice cream businesses should aim for -25°C

KKM Inspection Checklist for Cold Storage

When a KKM food safety officer inspects your cold storage facility, here is what they check:

Mandatory Items

  • Temperature display visible and readable from outside the cold room / on each freezer
  • Temperature log records available (manual or digital), showing checks at least twice daily
  • All temperature readings within range: frozen ≤ -18°C, chilled 0–4°C
  • No ice/frost buildup on evaporator coils, ceiling, or product
  • Food stored off the floor (minimum 15 cm / 6 inches clearance)
  • Adequate airflow — products not stacked against walls or blocking air vents
  • Cleanliness — no mould, no standing water, no food debris
  • Pest control — no evidence of rodents, cockroaches, or flies
  • FIFO system — First In, First Out, with date labels on all stock
  • Separate storage — raw and cooked foods stored separately; halal and non-halal separated
  • Staff access control — cold room not accessible to the public

Documentation Check

  • Temperature monitoring records (manual logs or data logger printouts)
  • Calibration certificates for thermometers (annually calibrated)
  • Corrective action records — what was done when temperature went out of range
  • Maintenance records for cooling equipment
  • Pest control service records

Red Flag Items (Immediate Failure)

  • Temperature above safe range with no corrective action taken
  • Products stored directly on the floor
  • Mould growth inside the cold room
  • Pest infestation
  • No temperature logs at all
  • Expired stock mixed with current stock

Thermometer Calibration

All thermometers and data loggers must be calibrated at least once a year. Keep the calibration certificate for KKM inspection.

Calibration services are available from:

  • SIRIM Calibration Laboratory (Shah Alam)
  • National Metrology Institute of Malaysia (NMIM)
  • Private calibration labs

Cost: RM50–200 per device.

Cold Room Design Basics

Insulation

MaterialR-Value (insulation)Typical ThicknessCost
Polyurethane (PU) panels7–8 per inch100–150 mmRM200–400/sq ft
Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) panels4–5 per inch150–200 mmRM120–250/sq ft
Rockwool (mineral wool)3–4 per inch200 mm+RM150–300/sq ft

PU panels are the industry standard for frozen food cold rooms. They offer the best insulation per centimetre of thickness, which saves space and reduces long-term electricity costs.

Shelving and Storage Layout

  • Material: Stainless steel or epoxy-coated wire shelving (not bare metal — it rusts)
  • Floor clearance: Bottom shelf at least 15 cm above floor
  • Wall clearance: Products at least 10–15 cm from walls for air circulation
  • Ceiling clearance: Do not stack products above the evaporator fan level
  • Aisle width: Minimum 80 cm for staff movement; 1.2 m+ for trolley/forklift access

Airflow Principles

Cold air must circulate freely to maintain uniform temperature throughout the room:

  1. Do not block the evaporator fan — keep the area directly in front of and below the fan clear
  2. Arrange shelving to create airflow lanes (parallel to airflow from the evaporator)
  3. Do not overfill — a cold room at 70–80% capacity cools more effectively than one packed to 100%
  4. Consider air circulation fans for larger rooms (>500 sq ft) to prevent hot spots

Door Management

Every time the cold room door opens, warm air enters. Consequences:

  • Temperature spike inside the cold room
  • Increased compressor workload and electricity consumption
  • Moisture ingress leading to frost buildup

Solutions:

  • Strip curtains (PVC strips over door) — RM200–500, reduce air exchange by 50–70%
  • Air curtain — RM1,500–4,000, creates an air barrier when door is open
  • Rapid roll-up doors — RM3,000–8,000, minimise open time
  • Door alarm — alerts if door is left open for more than 60 seconds

Delivery Temperature Checks

Frozen food must stay at ≤ -18°C throughout delivery. Here is the required protocol:

Before Loading

  1. Pre-cool the refrigerated truck to -20°C or below
  2. Log the truck temperature

At Loading

  1. Measure and log the core temperature of the product being loaded (probe thermometer inserted between packages)
  2. Minimise loading time — have stock organised and ready
  3. The truck’s refrigeration unit must run continuously during loading

During Delivery

  1. Avoid opening the truck door at every stop — group deliveries by zone
  2. Each door opening raises the temperature inside; allow 5–10 minutes recovery time between stops
  3. If using cooler boxes for last-mile delivery, pre-chill the boxes and use sufficient ice packs

At Delivery

  1. Check and log product temperature upon handover
  2. Have the customer sign acknowledging the temperature at delivery
  3. If temperature is above -15°C, document it and agree on acceptance/rejection with the customer

FAQ

What temperature should frozen chicken be stored at?

≤ -18°C, as mandated by the Food Hygiene Regulations 2009. This applies to all frozen poultry — whole chicken, chicken parts, boneless chicken, and processed chicken products. The core temperature of the product must remain at or below -18°C at all times during storage and delivery.

For long-term storage (over 3 months), some suppliers maintain -20°C to -22°C to preserve quality and account for temperature fluctuations. KKM inspectors will check both the cold room display temperature and the actual product core temperature during audits.

How long can frozen food be at room temperature during delivery?

Ideally, no time at all. Frozen food should remain at or below -18°C throughout the delivery process.

However, in practice, short exposures happen during loading and unloading. KKM guidelines are:

  • 30 minutes or less at room temperature (30°C) — generally acceptable if the product core temperature stays below -15°C and the product is returned to -18°C immediately
  • 1–2 hours — risky. The outer layer of the product may begin to thaw. If the core temperature exceeds -12°C, the product should ideally be used immediately or discarded rather than re-frozen, as partial thawing and refreezing causes quality degradation and increases food safety risk
  • Over 2 hours — the product should be considered compromised if the surface temperature has risen above 4°C (entering the danger zone). Discard or cook immediately

Best practice: Use refrigerated transport and pre-chilled cooler boxes. Never rely on the product staying frozen at room temperature — frozen food in Malaysia’s 30°C+ ambient temperature will begin thawing within 15–30 minutes.

Do I need a backup generator for my cold storage?

Strongly recommended, and effectively required for insurance and certification.

Here is why:

  • A power failure of 4+ hours can bring a walk-in cold room from -20°C to above -12°C, especially if the door has been opened
  • Once the temperature rises above -18°C, the food technically fails KKM compliance
  • Your entire stock — potentially tens of thousands of ringgit — is at risk
  • Most business interruption insurance policies require backup power as a condition of coverage

What if you don’t have a generator? At minimum, have a documented emergency plan:

  • Contacts for generator rental companies (24-hour service)
  • An agreement with a nearby cold storage facility to accept your stock in an emergency
  • Adequate business interruption insurance

For a KKM audit or MeSTI/HACCP certification, you will be asked: “What is your contingency plan for power failure?” Having a generator is the most straightforward answer.
