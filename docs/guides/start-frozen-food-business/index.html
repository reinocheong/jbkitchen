How to Start a Frozen Food Business in Malaysia: Complete Guide 2026

Why Start a Frozen Food Business in Malaysia?

The frozen food industry in Malaysia is growing rapidly. With rising demand from restaurants, caterers, convenience stores, and households, frozen food distribution offers stable margins and repeat business. Unlike fresh food, frozen products have longer shelf life (6–24 months), lower spoilage risk, and are not subject to daily market price swings.

Whether you want to supply local restaurants in Johor Bahru, distribute imported frozen products, or run a small cold storage operation from a shop lot — this guide covers every step.

Step 1: Choose Your Business Structure

Enterprise (Sole Proprietorship / Partnership)

Best for: Small-scale startups, home-based distributors, testing the market.

CriteriaDetails
Registration bodySSM (Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia)
Registration costRM30–60

Pros: Fast (1–3 working days), low cost, simple accounting. Cons: Unlimited liability — your personal assets are at risk if the business fails or is sued. Harder to get supplier credit terms and government contracts.

Sdn Bhd (Private Limited Company)

Best for: Medium to large operations, multi-outlet distribution, importing frozen food.

CriteriaDetails
Registration bodySSM (Companies Commission)
Registration costRM1,000–1,500 (incorporation)
Minimum paid-up capitalRM1
LiabilityLimited to paid-up capital
Ownership100% foreign ownership allowed (with conditions)
Tax rateCorporate tax (15–24%, depending on size)
Audit requirementMandatory annual audit

Pros: Limited liability, better credibility with suppliers and banks, easier to attract investors, can apply for more licenses (including AP for imports). Cons: Higher setup cost, annual compliance cost (secretary RM1,000–2,000/year, audit RM2,000–5,000/year), more paperwork.

Recommendation

Start with Enterprise if you are testing the market with capital under RM50k. Upgrade to Sdn Bhd once monthly turnover exceeds RM20k–30k, or immediately if you plan to import frozen food (AP license requires Sdn Bhd).

Step 2: SSM Registration Process

For Enterprise

  1. Visit any SSM counter or go to SSM ezbiz Online
  2. Conduct a name search (RM30–60 per name) — have 3 name options ready
  3. Fill in the Business Registration Form (Form A) — owner details, business address, business description
  4. Pay the registration fee (RM30 for sole proprietor, RM60 for partnership)
  5. Receive your Business Registration Certificate immediately

For Sdn Bhd

  1. Engage a company secretary (mandatory for incorporation)
  2. Conduct name search and reserve company name
  3. Prepare Constitution (formerly M&A) and incorporation documents
  4. Register directors (minimum 1, resident in Malaysia) and shareholders
  5. Submit to SSM through MyCoID portal
  6. Approval: 1–3 working days

Tip: Company secretary services cost RM1,000–2,000 for incorporation. Many firms offer package deals that include the first year of secretarial compliance.

Step 3: Required Licenses and Certifications

Operating a frozen food business in Malaysia requires several licenses. Do not skip any — KKM (Ministry of Health) and local councils conduct regular inspections.

1. Local Council Business License (PBT)

Mandatory for all businesses with a physical premises.

Apply at the local council where your premises is located:

  • Johor Bahru — Majlis Bandaraya Johor Bahru (MBJB)
  • Iskandar Puteri — Majlis Bandaraya Iskandar Puteri (MBIP)
  • Pasir Gudang — Majlis Perbandaran Pasir Gudang (MPPG)

Cost: RM100–500/year depending on premises size. Documents needed: SSM certificate, tenancy agreement, premises floor plan, fire safety certificate.

2. MeSTI Certification (KKM)

Mandatory for all food storage and distribution businesses.

MeSTI (Makanan Selamat Tanggungjawab Industri) is the minimum food safety certification required by the Ministry of Health. It covers:

  • Premises hygiene and layout
  • Cold storage temperature control
  • Staff training (food handling certificates)
  • Pest control and sanitation

Cost: RM200–500 (application), plus premises upgrades if needed. Timeline: 3–6 months from application to certification.

For full details, see our MeSTI Certification Guide.

3. HACCP Certification (KKM)

Required for larger-scale frozen food operations, especially if repackaging or processing frozen food.

HACCP is a step up from MeSTI. Many restaurants and supermarket chains require suppliers to be HACCP-certified.

Cost: RM5,000–15,000. Timeline: 3–6 months.

4. JAKIM Halal Certification

Highly recommended if supplying to Muslim-majority customers, restaurants, or retailers.

  • All products must come from JAKIM-certified suppliers
  • Your premises must have a Muslim supervisor
  • Storage must separate halal from non-halal

Cost: RM500–2,000. Timeline: 1–2 months.

See our JAKIM Halal Certification Guide for complete details.

5. Frozen Food Storage Permit (MAQIS / KKM)

If you store imported frozen food (meat, poultry, seafood), your cold storage facility may need a storage permit from the Malaysian Quarantine and Inspection Services (MAQIS) or KKM, depending on the product type.

6. Approved Permit (AP) — if importing

If you are importing frozen food (meat, poultry, certain seafood) into Malaysia, you need an Approved Permit (AP) from the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Industries (MAFI). This is a complex process — see our Frozen Food Import Permit Guide for full details.

Power Backup

A backup generator is strongly recommended. A power failure of more than 2–4 hours can bring your frozen stock above safe temperatures, causing total loss. A basic diesel generator for a small cold room costs RM5,000–15,000.

Step 6: Finding Suppliers

Local Frozen Food Suppliers

Local suppliers are easier to work with — lower minimum orders, faster delivery, no import paperwork.

Where to find them:

  • Trade directories — FMM (Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers) directory, MATRADE
  • Industry events — MIFB (Malaysian International Food & Beverage), Cafe & Restaurants Malaysia
  • Food wholesale markets — Selayang Wholesale Market (KL), Larkin Market (JB)
  • Referrals — restaurant owners, caterers, food manufacturers
  • Online — Alibaba (filter for Malaysian suppliers), marketing platforms

When evaluating a local supplier, check:

  • Halal certification — JAKIM certificate must be valid and cover the products you are buying
  • MeSTI / HACCP — confirms they follow food safety standards
  • Cold chain capability — refrigerated trucks, temperature logs
  • Minimum order quantity (MOQ) — can you meet their minimums?
  • Payment terms — net 30, net 60, consignment?
  • Delivery reliability — ask existing customers

Importing Frozen Food

Importing opens up a wider range of products and often lower costs, but comes with additional complexity:

  • Approved Permit (AP) required for meat, poultry, and certain seafood
  • Halal certification from the exporting country must be recognised by JAKIM
  • MAQIS quarantine inspection at the port of entry
  • Minimum container quantities — a 20ft container holds ~20,000 kg

For a complete walkthrough, see our Frozen Food Import Permit Guide.

Delivery Temperature Requirements

During transport, frozen food must remain at ≤ -18°C. You should:

  • Pre-cool the vehicle before loading
  • Check and log temperature at loading and at delivery
  • Minimise door opening time
  • Use temperature data loggers to prove cold chain integrity

Kkm officers may check delivery temperatures during roadside inspections.

Delivery Range Planning

Map out your delivery zones based on travel time, not distance:

  • Zone 1 (within 30 min) — daily delivery feasible
  • Zone 2 (30–60 min) — alternate day delivery
  • Zone 3 (1–2 hours) — 1–2 times per week
  • Zone 4 (2+ hours) — weekly or bulk delivery

Calculate your delivery cost per trip: fuel + driver time + vehicle depreciation. This determines your minimum order value per zone.

Step 8: Pricing Strategy

Cost-Plus Pricing

The simplest approach:

Selling Price = Landed Cost ÷ (1 - Target Margin %)

Example:
Landed cost: RM8.00/kg
Target margin: 30%
Selling price = 8.00 ÷ (1 - 0.30) = RM11.43/kg

Typical Margins by Product

Product CategoryTypical Wholesale Margin
Basic frozen (chicken, fish fillets)15–25%
Processed frozen (nuggets, sausages)20–30%
Premium / imported frozen25–40%
Frozen ready meals30–50%

Pricing Factors to Consider

  • Your operating costs — cold storage electricity, delivery, staff, rent
  • Competitor pricing — what are other wholesalers in your area charging?
  • Customer type — restaurants expect wholesale pricing; retail customers accept higher prices
  • Volume discounts — offer tiered pricing for bulk orders
  • Payment terms — cash-and-carry can be cheaper than credit terms (risk + working capital cost)

Minimum Order Value

Set a minimum order value to ensure each delivery is profitable. For a refrigerated delivery costing RM30–50 per trip, a minimum order of RM200–300 is reasonable.

FAQ

Do I need halal certification to sell frozen food?

It depends on your target market. If you sell only to non-halal restaurants (e.g., Chinese restaurants serving pork), halal certification is not mandatory. However, if you want to supply to:

  • Muslim-majority restaurants
  • Supermarkets and hypermarkets
  • Government institutions (schools, hospitals)
  • Major food service companies

Then yes, halal certification is practically essential. Without it, you lock out the majority of the Malaysian food market. Additionally, JAKIM halal certification on your frozen food products significantly increases consumer trust. Even if you are currently supplying only non-halal outlets, plan for halal certification as you scale.

How much capital do I need to start?

Small-scale: RM30,000–50,000. This covers an Enterprise registration, 2–3 chest freezers in a rented shop lot or at home, initial stock, basic packaging, and a second-hand vehicle with cooler boxes for delivery.

Medium-scale: RM80,000–150,000. This covers a Sdn Bhd setup, a small walk-in cold room, 5–10 chest freezers, proper temperature monitoring, a refrigerated delivery van, backup generator, and 3 months of working capital.

What you cannot cut corners on: cold storage (to keep food safe), business registration and licenses (to operate legally), and food safety certification (MeSTI is mandatory). If your budget is tight, start smaller — one chest freezer, one product category, one delivery zone — and expand as cash flow builds.

Can I run a frozen food business from home?

Technically yes, but with significant restrictions.

  • Your home kitchen or garage must meet MeSTI requirements: smooth washable surfaces, no pets in the storage area, separate hand-washing basin, proper ventilation, pest control
  • You need a business license from your local council (PBT) — and not all councils approve food businesses in residential zones. Check with MBJB/MPJB before renting or investing
  • If you are in a strata property (condo, apartment), the management body and house rules may prohibit commercial food storage
  • Neighbours may complain — delivery trucks, generator noise, increased traffic

Practical approach: Start from home with 1–2 chest freezers while testing the market. Once you have consistent orders (3–6 months of stable revenue), move to a shop lot. A small shop lot in JB costs RM800–2,000/month and avoids the compliance headaches of running from home.
