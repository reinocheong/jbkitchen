Frozen Food Import Permit Malaysia: AP License Guide

Overview: Malaysia’s Import Control System for Frozen Food

Importing frozen food into Malaysia is a regulated process. The government controls imports through a system of Approved Permits (AP) , licensing, and inspection to protect:

  • Domestic food producers — price stability and market access for local farmers
  • Food safety — preventing contaminated or substandard products from entering the food supply
  • Halal integrity — ensuring imported meat and poultry meet JAKIM halal standards
  • Biosecurity — preventing animal diseases and pests through MAQIS quarantine

This guide covers the complete import process, the agencies involved, costs, and timelines — based on real-world experience importing through Malaysian ports.

Approved Permit (AP) for Frozen Food

What Is an AP?

An Approved Permit (AP) is a government-issued licence that allows the holder to import specific categories of goods into Malaysia. It is not optional — if your product is on the controlled list, importing without an AP is an offence under the Customs Act 1967.

Key rule: Fresh, chilled, and frozen meat and poultry almost always require an AP. Processed food products generally do not, unless they contain a regulated meat component above a certain threshold.

To check whether your specific product requires an AP, consult the Customs (Prohibition of Imports) Order or contact MAFI directly.

Who Can Apply for an AP?

APs are not issued to just anyone. Eligibility requirements include:

  • Registered company in Malaysia (Sdn Bhd strongly preferred; Enterprise may face difficulty)
  • Minimum paid-up capital of RM250,000–500,000 (varies by product category — MAFI sets specific requirements)
  • Bumiputera equity — certain AP categories under MAFI have Bumiputera ownership requirements
  • Physical premises with cold storage facility (MeSTI or HACCP certified)
  • Track record in food distribution or related industry
  • No history of AP violations or customs offences

How to Apply for an AP

  1. Identify the correct AP category for your product — contact MAFI’s AP Unit or visit their website: https://www.mafi.gov.my
  2. Prepare documentation: SSM certificate, company profile, cold storage details, audited accounts (last 2 years), product list, supplier details from the exporting country
  3. Submit application through the ePermit system (linked to Dagang Net) or directly to MAFI
  4. Wait for review and approval — typically 4–8 weeks, but can be longer during peak application periods
  5. Pay the AP fee upon approval (varies by product and volume)

Realistic assessment: Obtaining a new AP for frozen meat/poultry is challenging for first-time applicants. Many importers begin by:

  • Partnering with an existing AP holder (acting as a distributor rather than direct importer)
  • Importing products that do not require an AP (frozen vegetables, processed foods, seafood fillets)
  • Applying for an AP once they have a track record of food distribution and certified cold storage

AP Validity and Renewal

  • APs are typically valid for one year from the date of issue
  • APs are linked to a specific volume or value of imports
  • Renewal must be applied for before expiry, with updated documentation
  • Failure to use the AP (no imports) may result in non-renewal

Customs Declaration Process

The K1 Form

All imports into Malaysia must be declared using the Customs Form K1 (Electronic Declaration). This is submitted through the Dagang Net electronic system, usually by a licensed customs broker.

Import Duty Rates

Import duty is calculated as a percentage of the CIF value (Cost + Insurance + Freight) in RM.

ASEAN Free Trade Area (AFTA) : If you import from ASEAN countries (e.g., Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia), many frozen food products qualify for 0% duty under the Common Effective Preferential Tariff (CEPT) scheme. You must provide a Form D (Certificate of Origin) from the exporting country to claim this preference.

SST on Imported Frozen Food

Sales Tax (SST) may apply on imported frozen food at the point of import:

  • Basic food items (frozen meat, fish, vegetables) — generally exempt from sales tax
  • Processed food products (frozen ready meals, breaded products, frozen desserts) — may be subject to 5–10% sales tax
  • Check the Sales Tax (Goods Exempted From Sales Tax) Order for your specific HS code

Customs Declaration Step by Step

  1. Engage a licensed customs broker — they will handle the declaration for you (fee: RM200–500 per declaration)
  2. Provide the broker with: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading / airway bill, insurance certificate, certificate of origin, halal certificate, health certificate
  3. Broker submits the K1 form electronically through Dagang Net
  4. RMCD processes the declaration — 1–3 working days for standard clearance
  5. Pay any import duty and SST assessed
  6. Receive customs clearance — container is released for MAQIS inspection

Halal Import Requirements

For Meat and Poultry

Malaysia has strict halal import requirements for meat and poultry:

  • The foreign abattoir / processing plant must be approved by JAKIM — not just the product, but the facility itself
  • The product must carry a halal certificate from a JAKIM-recognised certifying body in the exporting country
  • JAKIM maintains a list of approved foreign halal certification bodies and approved abattoirs. Importing from a non-listed source means your shipment will be rejected

Countries with JAKIM-Recognised Halal Meat Facilities

JAKIM has recognised halal certification bodies and approved slaughterhouses in:

  • Australia, New Zealand, India, Brazil, Thailand, Indonesia, China (specific provinces)
  • Several EU countries under bilateral agreements

The full list is maintained on the JAKIM Halal Hub. Always check before committing to a supplier — the list is updated regularly.

Documentation Required at Import

  • Halal certificate from the recognised foreign certification body (must match the shipment details)
  • Islamic slaughter certificate for meat products
  • Health certificate from the veterinary authority in the exporting country

Halal documentation is verified by JAKIM officers stationed at designated entry points. If documents are incomplete or the source is not on the approved list, the shipment is detained.

MAQIS Quarantine Inspection

MAQIS (Malaysian Quarantine and Inspection Services) inspects all imported animal and plant products at the point of entry. This is a separate step from customs clearance.

MAQIS Inspection Process

  1. Permit application: Before the shipment arrives, your broker applies for a MAQIS import permit (separate from the AP)
  2. Document check: MAQIS reviews health certificates, veterinary certificates, and import permits
  3. Physical inspection: MAQIS officers open and inspect a percentage of the shipment — checking temperature, packaging integrity, labelling, and product condition
  4. Sampling (if required): Samples may be taken for laboratory testing (additional cost: RM50–200 per test)
  5. Release or detain: If compliant, MAQIS issues a release order. If issues are found, the shipment is detained pending corrective action or destruction

Reefer Container Logistics

Frozen food is shipped in reefer (refrigerated) containers. Key considerations:

  • Reefer plug-in fee at the port: RM50–150/day (after free period, typically 3–5 days)
  • Free storage period before demurrage charges begin: varies by shipping line (3–7 days). Plan customs clearance to stay within this window
  • Generator set (genset) for road transport from port to your cold storage: RM200–500 per trip
  • Container deposit: RM3,000–10,000 (refundable when the empty container is returned to the depot)

FAQ

Which frozen foods need an AP?

Meat and poultry almost always need an AP. This includes frozen beef, mutton, lamb, chicken, duck, and turkey (whole, parts, or boneless). Some whole fish species also require an AP, depending on MAFI’s current controlled list.

Generally no AP needed for: frozen processed seafood (fillets, prawns, squid), frozen vegetables, frozen fruits, frozen ready meals without regulated meat content, frozen french fries, frozen bakery products.

The definitive list is in the Customs (Prohibition of Imports) Order, updated periodically. Before placing an order with any overseas supplier, verify the AP requirement for your product’s HS code with MAFI, your customs broker, or both.

Can I import frozen food from China?

Yes, but with important caveats for meat and poultry:

  • Halal meat from China — China has JAKIM-approved halal slaughterhouses, but the list is limited. Verify that your specific supplier’s facility is on the JAKIM-approved list before committing to an order
  • Non-halal products (e.g., frozen vegetables, seafood, processed foods) — generally fewer restrictions, but you must still comply with KKM food safety standards and labelling requirements
  • AP requirement — If importing regulated products (meat, poultry), the AP requirement applies regardless of the country of origin, including China
  • Documentation — Ensure all health certificates, certificates of origin, and halal certificates (if applicable) are issued by the relevant Chinese authorities and comply with Malaysian requirements
  • Quality concerns — Some Chinese frozen food imports have faced scrutiny in Malaysia. Ensure your supplier meets international cold chain standards and can provide temperature monitoring data

In practice, importing frozen vegetables, processed seafood, and non-meat frozen products from China is relatively straightforward (no AP, low duty). Importing meat and poultry requires the full AP, JAKIM, and MAQIS process.

How long does customs clearance take for frozen food?

Standard clearance: 5–10 working days from vessel arrival to release, assuming:

  • All documentation is complete and correct
  • The HS code is accurate
  • The AP is valid and has sufficient remaining quota
  • Halal documentation is in order (for meat/poultry)
  • MAQIS inspection does not flag issues

With complications: 2–4 weeks, if any of the following occur:

  • MAQIS selects the shipment for laboratory testing
  • Halal documentation needs verification with JAKIM
  • The HS code is queried by Customs
  • The AP quantity needs reconciliation

Worst case (detention): If the shipment is detained for compliance issues, resolution can take 1–3 months. The product remains in the reefer container at the port during this time — you continue paying plug-in fees throughout. A detained reefer shipment can cost RM3,000–5,000+ in additional port charges alone.

To minimise clearance time:

  • Work with a customs broker who specialises in frozen food imports (not a generalist broker)
  • Submit all documents before the vessel arrives (pre-clearance)
  • Ensure your AP has sufficient remaining quantity for the shipment
  • Double-check halal certificates against JAKIM’s current recognised facility list
  • Budget 2 weeks for clearance in your inventory planning — a 10-day clearance should not cause a stock-out

What is the difference between MAQIS and Customs clearance?

Customs (RMCD) handles: import duty assessment, SST collection, documentation compliance (K1 form, invoice, packing list), verification of AP validity. Customs clearance means you have met all fiscal and documentary requirements — the goods can legally enter Malaysia from a tax and trade perspective.

MAQIS handles: quarantine and biosecurity inspection — checking the physical condition of the imported animal/plant products, verifying health and veterinary certificates, inspecting for pests and diseases. MAQIS clearance means the goods are safe to enter the Malaysian food supply from a biological perspective.

You need both clearances. They are separate processes, handled by different agencies, and often happen sequentially (Customs first, then MAQIS physical inspection). Your customs broker coordinates both.

Do I need an import licence if I am buying from a Malaysian importer instead of importing directly?

No. If you buy frozen food from a Malaysian company that has already imported and cleared the goods (an importer or wholesaler), you are simply purchasing from a local supplier. You do not need an AP, do not deal with customs, and do not interact with MAQIS. The importing company has already handled all of that.

This is by far the most common way for small and medium frozen food businesses to access imported products. You pay a markup to the importer (typically 15–30% above landed cost), but you avoid:

  • The complexity of AP applications
  • Customs and MAQIS clearance
  • Container-sized minimum order quantities
  • Currency risk and international logistics

For most small frozen food distributors and restaurants, buying from a local importer is the practical choice. Consider direct importing only when your monthly volume for a specific product exceeds half a container (10,000+ kg/month) — at that scale, the cost savings outweigh the complexity.
