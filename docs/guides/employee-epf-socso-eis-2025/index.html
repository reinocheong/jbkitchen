Employer EPF SOCSO EIS 2025: Restaurant & F&B Guide

Overview: Statutory Contributions for F&B Employers

If you run a restaurant, catering business, frozen food wholesale operation, or any food-related business in Malaysia with employees, you are legally required to make monthly statutory contributions for every eligible worker. The three mandatory contributions are:

ContributionAgencyPurpose
EPFKWSP (Kumpulan Wang Simpanan Pekerja)Retirement savings
SOCSOPERKESO (Pertubuhan Keselamatan Sosial)Employment injury & invalidity insurance
EISSOCSO / SIP (Sistem Insurans Pekerjaan)Unemployment insurance

Failure to pay or late payment attracts significant penalties, and PERKESO and KWSP officers actively audit F&B businesses. This guide covers the exact rates, registration process, payment deadlines, and what to watch out for.

EPF (KWSP) — Employees Provident Fund

Contribution Rates (Effective 2025)

Employee Monthly WageEmployer ShareEmployee ShareTotal
RM5,000 and below13%11%24%
Above RM5,00012%11%23%

Key points:

  • These rates are for Malaysian citizens and permanent residents under the standard EPF scheme
  • The employer share of 13% (for wages RM5k and below) has been in effect since 2024 and continues into 2025
  • Employees can voluntarily contribute more than 11% (up to 100% of wages) — file with KWSP using Form KWSP 17A (Khas)
  • Contributions are calculated based on total monthly wages: basic salary + fixed allowances + overtime + commission + bonus

Example: EPF Calculation

Employee monthly wage: RM3,000

Employer share:  13% × RM3,000 = RM390.00
Employee share:  11% × RM3,000 = RM330.00
Total EPF:       RM720.00

Employee take-home before deductions:
RM3,000 - RM330 (EPF) - RM15 (SOCSO) - RM6 (EIS) = RM2,649

EPF Contribution for Wages Above RM20,000

For employees earning above RM20,000 per month, EPF contributions are calculated on wages up to RM20,000 only. The excess above RM20,000 is not subject to EPF.

Example: Salary RM25,000

EPF-able wages: RM20,000
Employer share:  12% × RM20,000 = RM2,400
Employee share:  11% × RM20,000 = RM2,200

SOCSO (PERKESO) — Social Security Organisation

SOCSO has two separate schemes that employers must contribute to:

Scheme 1: Employment Injury Scheme (EIS — not to be confused with Employment Insurance)

  • Covers workplace accidents, commuting accidents, and occupational diseases
  • Employer pays 100% of this contribution — no employee deduction
  • The rate depends on your industry classification

Scheme 2: Invalidity Pension Scheme (IPS)

  • Covers invalidity (disability preventing work) and death from any cause
  • Shared: employer + employee contributions

SOCSO Ceiling

The maximum insured monthly wage for SOCSO is RM5,000 (effective from 2022). For employees earning above RM5,000, SOCSO contributions are calculated as if they earned RM5,000.

EIS (SIP) — Employment Insurance System

The Employment Insurance System (EIS) — also called SIP (Sistem Insurans Pekerjaan) — provides temporary financial assistance to workers who lose their jobs. It is managed by PERKESO.

EIS Contribution Rates

RateMonthly Cap
Employer share0.2% of monthly wageRM10.40
Employee share0.2% of monthly wageRM10.40
Total0.4%RM20.80

The insured wage ceiling for EIS is RM5,000/month. Contributions on wages above RM5,000 are calculated as if the wage was RM5,000.

Example: EIS Calculation

Employee monthly wage: RM2,500

Employer: 0.2% × RM2,500 = RM5.00
Employee: 0.2% × RM2,500 = RM5.00
Total EIS: RM10.00

Employee monthly wage: RM8,000 (capped at RM5,000)

Employer: 0.2% × RM5,000 = RM10.00
Employee: 0.2% × RM5,000 = RM10.00
Total EIS: RM20.00

Who Is Exempt from EIS?

  • Employees aged 57 and above (no EIS contribution required)
  • Domestic workers
  • Self-employed individuals
  • Civil servants (pensionable)

HRDF / PSMB Levy — Human Resources Development Fund

If your F&B business has 10 or more Malaysian employees, you must register with the Human Resource Development Corporation (HRD Corp) and pay the HRDF levy.

CriteriaRate
10+ Malaysian employees1% of total monthly wages
5–9 Malaysian employees (voluntary)0.5% of total monthly wages

What counts as “employee”? All Malaysian citizens on your payroll, including full-time, part-time, contract, and probationary staff.

What does HRDF fund? Training programmes for your staff. You can claim back training costs from the levy you pay.

Registration is done at HRD Corp Portal.

Penalty for non-registration: Fine up to RM10,000 or imprisonment up to 1 year, or both.

Foreign Worker Levies and Contributions

EPF for Foreign Workers

Foreign workers are not required to contribute to EPF (neither employer nor employee share). However:

  • Employers may voluntarily contribute RM5/month to EPF for foreign workers (optional)
  • If you want to make EPF contributions for a foreign worker, register them under a separate account

SOCSO for Foreign Workers

Yes, foreign workers must be covered by SOCSO. The Employment Injury Scheme applies. Contribution rates are similar to local employees.

EIS for Foreign Workers

No. Foreign workers are not covered under EIS. Do not deduct or contribute EIS for foreign workers.

Registration Process — New Employers

Step 1: Register with SSM

Before anything else, your business must be registered with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM). You will need your SSM registration number for all subsequent registrations.

Step 2: Register with KWSP (EPF)

  1. Go to the KWSP i-Akaun Employer Portal
  2. Select “Employer Registration”
  3. Provide: SSM registration number, employer details, company bank account
  4. Submit and receive your EPF Employer Code within 1–3 working days
  5. Register all employees who are EPF contributors (Malaysian citizens and permanent residents)

Step 3: Register with PERKESO (SOCSO + EIS)

  1. Go to the PERKESO ASSIST Portal
  2. Select “Employer Registration”
  3. Provide: SSM registration number, employer details, industry classification (Category 18 for F&B)
  4. Submit and receive your Employer Code within 1–3 working days
  5. Register all employees (including foreign workers for SOCSO)

Registration for SOCSO and EIS is done through the same portal. EIS was added to the PERKESO system in 2018.

Step 4: Register with LHDN (PCB — Income Tax Deduction)

If your employees earn above the taxable threshold (approximately RM3,400/month after EPF deduction), you must register as an employer with LHDN (Inland Revenue Board) and deduct monthly PCB (Potongan Cukai Bulanan) from their wages.

  1. Go to the e-PCB / e-Data portal
  2. Register for an E Number (Employer Tax Reference Number)
  3. Calculate monthly PCB deductions using the PCB Schedule or e-Calculator on the LHDN website

Penalties for Late Payment

ContributionPenalty
EPF late paymentPenalty calculated on a daily basis at the dividend rate declared by KWSP for that year
SOCSO late payment6% simple interest per annum on the outstanding amount, calculated daily
EIS late paymentSame as SOCSO (6% per annum)
PCB late payment10% penalty on the unpaid amount; further 5% after 60 days

Additional penalties:

  • PERKESO may issue a compound of up to RM5,000 for failure to register as an employer
  • KWSP can take legal action to recover unpaid contributions, including civil suits and travel bans on directors
  • Late or non-payment of EPF by the 15th is automatically flagged in the KWSP system and compounded
  • Repeat offenders face prosecution

Important: The EPF penalty applies even if you are one day late. KWSP does not provide a grace period — the penalty clock starts on the 16th.

Special Considerations for the F&B Industry

Part-Time and Casual Workers

EPF, SOCSO, and EIS apply to all employees who earn wages, regardless of employment type:

  • Part-time workers — yes, must contribute
  • Casual workers — yes, must contribute
  • Probationary employees — yes, must contribute
  • Interns receiving an allowance — possibly (depends on nature of payment — check with KWSP if the allowance is structured as wages)
  • Family members working in the business drawing a salary — yes, must contribute

There is no minimum earning threshold for EPF, SOCSO, or EIS. If you pay someone wages, you contribute.

Restaurant Tipping and Service Charge

The treatment of service charge is a common point of confusion in the restaurant industry:

  • Service charge collected from customers — if the full amount is distributed to employees as wages, it forms part of their monthly wages for EPF/SOCSO/EIS calculation purposes
  • Cash tips given directly to staff by customers — these are not considered wages for statutory contributions (difficult to track and not processed through the employer’s payroll)
  • Tip pooling and distribution — if tips are pooled by the employer and distributed, they may be considered wages. Consult your accountant or KWSP for a definitive ruling

Kitchen vs Service Staff

There is no difference in contribution rates between kitchen staff and service staff — all are employees under the same Category 18 classification. The only distinction that matters is:

  • Malaysian vs foreign worker — different EPF rules apply
  • Age 57+ vs below 57 — EIS exemption applies

FAQ

Do part-time staff need EPF?

Yes. EPF, SOCSO, and EIS contributions are mandatory for all employees who earn wages, regardless of whether they work full-time, part-time, on a contract basis, or are in a probationary period. There is no minimum wage threshold for EPF contributions — if you pay someone RM100 per week, you must contribute EPF (13% employer, 11% employee) on that RM100.

Many small F&B operators make the mistake of treating part-time workers as freelancers to avoid statutory contributions. This is non-compliant. If the worker reports to your premises at scheduled times, uses your equipment, and follows your instructions, they are an employee in the eyes of KWSP and PERKESO — regardless of what your verbal agreement calls them.

How do I register as a new employer for EPF and SOCSO?

EPF (KWSP):

  1. Go to https://iakaun.kwsp.gov.my
  2. Click “Employer Registration”
  3. Fill in your SSM registration number, business details, and contact information
  4. Provide your company’s bank account details
  5. Once approved (1–3 working days), you receive your KWSP Employer Code (e.g., 12345678-A)
  6. Log in to the i-Akaun employer portal and add your employees

SOCSO + EIS (PERKESO):

  1. Go to https://assist.perkeso.gov.my
  2. Click “Employer Registration”
  3. Provide your SSM number, business details, and select Industry Code 18 (food and beverage service activities)
  4. Upload a copy of your SSM certificate
  5. Once approved, receive your PERKESO Employer Code
  6. Register each employee through the same portal

Important: You must register with KWSP and PERKESO within 30 days of hiring your first employee. Late registration attracts penalties.

What if my employee is a foreign worker?

EPF: Foreign workers are exempt from mandatory EPF contributions. Neither you nor the foreign worker is required to contribute. You may voluntarily contribute RM5/month on behalf of the worker if you wish, but this is optional.

SOCSO: Foreign workers must be covered under SOCSO’s Employment Injury Scheme. Contribution rates are similar to those for Malaysian employees (approximately 1.25% employer share). Foreign workers are not covered under the Invalidity Pension Scheme.

EIS: Foreign workers are not eligible for EIS coverage. Do not deduct or contribute EIS for foreign workers.

Foreign Worker Levy: In addition to statutory contributions, you pay an annual levy of RM1,850 per worker for the services sector (which includes F&B). This is paid to the Immigration Department, not KWSP/PERKESO.

Do I need to contribute EPF for myself as the business owner?

If you are a sole proprietor (Enterprise), you are not legally required to contribute EPF for yourself. However, you can voluntarily contribute to EPF through the Self-Employed EPF scheme (i-Saraan). The government provides an annual incentive of up to RM300 for i-Saraan contributors.

If you are a director of a Sdn Bhd and draw a salary from the company, you are treated as an employee for EPF purposes and the standard contribution rates apply (13%/11% on director’s salary RM5k and below).

Do SOCSO rates change every year?

SOCSO contribution rates and wage ceilings are adjusted periodically by the government. The maximum insured wage ceiling has been increased over time (from RM3,000 to RM4,000 to the current RM5,000). Always check the PERKESO website for the latest contribution table. The rates shown in this guide are accurate as of 2025 but may change with future government announcements.
